Spooky & Odd Florida

Haunted dolls, gravity hills, psychic towns, skunk apes and a castle in a swamp. The creepiest and strangest real places you can visit in Florida. Pictures are illustrations; follow each link for real details, and check hours before you drive.

Haunted · Key West

Robert the Doll

A straw-stuffed doll in a little sailor suit who once belonged to Key West artist Robert Eugene Otto, and who, legend says, curses anyone who photographs him without asking first. The wall of apology letters from visitors suggests a lot of people forgot to ask.

On display at Fort East Martello Museum, run by the Key West Art & Historical Society. Ask nicely before you take his picture.

Mystery · Homestead

Coral Castle

One small man, Latvian immigrant Ed Leedskalnin, carved and moved more than 1,000 tons of coral rock by himself, mostly at night, for a sweetheart who never showed up. Nobody saw how he did it. He said he knew the secrets of the pyramids. Florida said, sure, Ed.

Coral Castle Museum & Sculpture Garden, Homestead. Check the museum's site for hours and tours.

Read more on Coral Castle Museum ↗
Mystery · Lake Wales

Spook Hill

Stop at the white line, shift into neutral and your car appears to roll uphill. Scientists call it an optical illusion. Lake Wales put up an official sign and leaned all the way in. Either way, it's the only Florida hill where traffic flows the wrong direction on purpose.

North Wales Drive, Lake Wales, a public residential street. Go slow and watch for neighbors.

Read more on FOX 13 ↗
Haunted · Cassadaga

Cassadaga Spiritualist Camp

A tiny Volusia County town founded in the 1890s by Spiritualists, where many residents are mediums and the main business is talking to the dead. They call it the Psychic Capital of the World. The rest of us call it the only town where the receptionist might already know why you're there.

Southern Cassadaga Spiritualist Camp, off I-4 between Orlando and Daytona. Readings, a bookstore and walking tours; it's a residential community, so be respectful.

Read more on AFAR ↗
Strange · Key West

Key West Cemetery

The only cemetery where the headstones do stand-up. The most famous one reads "I told you I was sick." Above-ground tombs, roaming chickens and some of the most Key West last words ever carved in stone.

Old Town Key West, entrance on Angela and Margaret streets. Free to walk; it's an active cemetery, so keep it respectful.

Read more on WLRN ↗
Cryptid · Ochopee

Skunk Ape Research Headquarters

Florida's Bigfoot is said to be seven feet tall and smell like a dumpster behind a fish market. This roadside HQ in the Everglades is run by its most devoted hunter, with a gift shop, swamp critters and plenty of theories about what's out there.

On the Tamiami Trail (U.S. 41) in Ochopee, Big Cypress country. Check before you go.

Read more on Atlas Obscura ↗
Strange · Ona

Solomon's Castle

A full-size castle in the middle of a Hardee County swamp, shiny with discarded aluminum printing plates and built over decades by artist Howard Solomon. There's a moat. There's a replica ship. There's a lot of puns. It's the most Florida thing Florida ever forgot to tell you about.

In rural Ona, Hardee County, about an hour east of Sarasota. Check hours and tour times before the drive.

Read more on Sarasota Magazine ↗
Mystery · Gainesville

The Devil's Millhopper

A giant sinkhole about 120 feet deep, with little waterfalls trickling down its sides and a rainforest at the bottom. Old stories say the fossils and bones found down there were the devil's leftovers. The state park just calls it a geology lesson with a lot of stairs.

Devil's Millhopper Geological State Park, Gainesville. A boardwalk staircase goes to the bottom.

Read more on Florida State Parks ↗
Strange · Biscayne Bay, Miami

Stiltsville

A handful of wooden houses standing on stilts in the middle of Biscayne Bay, leftovers of a once-rowdy offshore hangout that started in the 1930s. No roads, no neighbors, just water and the Miami skyline. It's a ghost town that floats. Sort of.

Inside Biscayne National Park; reachable only by boat. The park and its partners run guided tours.
