Where to Eat (and Where the Roaches Ate First): Florida's Week in Health Inspections

17 restaurants got shut down by the state this week. Over a thousand others aced it. Here's the scoreboard.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Statewide · Nov 25, 2026 · 2 min read

The quick take

Florida health inspectors ordered 17 restaurants to close temporarily in the week ending Nov. 22, mostly over roaches, rodents and flies; every one of them has since been cleared to reopen. Meanwhile, more than 1,100 Florida restaurants passed a routine inspection with zero violations in the last 30 days. Here's who got shut down, and a couple of spotless places worth a visit.

Every week, the State of Florida quietly publishes a list of restaurants it had to shut down. And every week, we read it so you don't have to read it while holding a fork.

This week's count: 17 emergency closures across the state, according to the Florida Division of Hotels and Restaurants. The good news: every single one has been cleared to reopen. The less good news: the reasons.

Who got shut down

Flies led the pack this week, with five closures for fly activity alone. Roaches were close behind, and rodents showed up in another handful of kitchens, sometimes bringing friends.

Pinellas County had the busiest week, with four St. Petersburg spots on the list: Halong Bay Vietnamese Cuisine (rodent, roach and fly activity, the full trifecta), Siam Garden Thai Restaurant (rodent activity), Mi Carreta Restaurant & Bakery (fly activity) and Pofolks (roach activity).

Miami-Dade had four too: Popeyes 2563 in Miami (flies), Mo's Bagel & Deli in Aventura (rodents and flies), Dicrespo in Miami (roaches and flies) and a restaurant licensed as McDonald of Fla Corp in Opa Locka (rodents and flies).

Elsewhere: El Patio in Fort Myers and Panda Rest in Wauchula (roaches), Nuevo Asados Al Carbon in Tampa (rodents), Casa Dora Italian Cafe in Jacksonville and Simon Parilla Bar and Grill in Longwood (roaches and rodents), Sabor Hispano in Margate (roaches), and Odd Birds Cocktail Lounge and Kitchen in St. Augustine, Sal's Italian Ristorante in Palm Beach Gardens and El Asador in Pensacola (flies).

A quick fairness note: a closure is a snapshot of one bad day, not a conviction. All of these places passed a follow-up visit and were allowed to reopen. Casa Dora, for one, bounced back with a clean inspection two days later.

Where to eat instead

Now the fun part. In the last 30 days, more than 1,100 Florida restaurants passed a routine state inspection with zero violations. Not one. That's harder than it sounds. Here are two that aced it this week.

Kinderhook Raw Bar & Crab Co. at Sunseeker Resort in Charlotte Harbor is a seafood counter in the resort's Harbor Yards food hall, with an ice bar of oysters, cracked crab and shellfish. Its own page shows off a lobster roll with fries, which is exactly the kind of thing you want to eat from a kitchen the state just called spotless.

Steaming Pots West Africa Cuisine in Tampa serves West African food, and its weekend breakfast includes African egg stew with yam. Delivery reviewers keep bringing up the egusi soup and puff puff, if you need a place to start.
