Florida Man Allegedly Poaches an 8-Point Buck, Then Posts the Video Online

FWC says the Plant City content creator reported the deer as a public-land harvest. His own footage had other ideas.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Sumter County · Nov 27, 2026 · 2 min read

The quick take

The Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission says a 41-year-old Plant City content creator harvested an eight-point buck while trespassing on private property in Sumter County, then filed a harvest report claiming he took it legally on public land. Officers got a tip on Oct. 26, reviewed a video he had posted of himself with the deer, and charged him on Nov. 20 with armed trespass and providing false information.

There are two golden rules of getting away with something. Rule one: don't do it. Rule two, for people who skipped rule one: whatever you do, don't film it and post it for the whole internet.

The Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission says a 41-year-old Plant City man who makes content online went zero for two.

The buck

According to an FWC news release, officers received information on Oct. 26 that the man had illegally harvested a male white-tailed deer with eight antler points. The problem, FWC says, was where he got it: on private property in Sumter County, where he was allegedly trespassing.

The man then posted a video of himself with the eight-point buck online. Which, to be fair, is what content creators do. It is also what investigators love.

The paperwork

In Florida, hunters have to log a deer harvest before moving the animal and report it within 24 hours, no matter where it was taken. FWC says the man did file a harvest report. He just allegedly filled it out with a little creative writing, saying the buck was legally taken on public land.

Officers reviewed the video and investigated. FWC says that work backed up the original tip and turned up additional evidence showing the deer was not harvested where he reported. The agency did not spell out what that evidence was, but it is safe to say the video did not help his case.

Mock-serious note to every aspiring outdoors influencer: the comment section is not the only audience. Sometimes the people watching have badges.

The charges

On Nov. 20, FWC officers charged the man with trespass on property other than a structure or conveyance while armed with a firearm, and providing false information. The agency describes it as one felony and one misdemeanor. In Florida, trespassing on land while armed is generally a third-degree felony, which carries up to five years in prison. FWC says a warrant was issued and he was taken to jail without incident. No bond or court date was listed.

FWC Lt. Michael Rice did not hold back, calling the alleged trespassing, false records and video "a serious lack of judgement and blatant disregard for the law." He added that the agency will keep holding violators accountable.

Why it matters

Florida's hunting rules aren't just red tape. Harvest reports help wildlife managers track deer numbers, and landowners have the right to decide who hunts on their property. Lying on the form undercuts both, which is why FWC treats it as a crime and not a typo.

So the buck is no longer on the hoof, the video is evidence and the man's next post may need to be a court calendar. He is presumed innocent until proven guilty. But if he is looking for content, he certainly has some now.
