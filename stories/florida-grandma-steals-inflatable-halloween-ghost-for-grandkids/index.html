Florida Grandma Allegedly Steals a $15 Inflatable Ghost Because the Grandkids Wanted It

Polk County deputies say the decoration tour came with a bonus stop. The bonus stop came with four charges.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Lakeland, Polk County · Nov 6, 2026 · 1 min read

The quick take

Polk County deputies say a 53-year-old Lakeland woman unplugged a $15 inflatable ghost from a stranger's porch on Oct. 17 while driving her three grandchildren around to look at Halloween decorations. She told deputies the grandkids wanted it. She returned the ghost and faces four charges, including contributing to the delinquency of a child.

Some grandmas bake cookies. Some knit sweaters. And one Lakeland grandma, deputies say, went full Grinch on a porch decoration two weeks before Halloween.

The target: an inflatable ghost. The value, according to its owner: about $15. The getaway vehicle: a car full of grandchildren.

The heist

On Oct. 17, a home security camera in Lakeland caught a woman walking up to a front porch, unplugging an inflatable ghost and walking off with it, according to the Polk County Sheriff's Office. Sheriff Grady Judd later shared the video.

No ski mask. No crowbar. Just a woman, a ghost and a very determined stride.

The explanation

When deputies caught up with her, the 53-year-old told them she had been driving three of her grandchildren, two 8-year-olds and a 9-year-old, around to look at Halloween decorations. The kids wanted the ghost, she said. So she got them the ghost.

It's the kind of logic that works great at a toy store. It works less great on a stranger's porch.

Judd was not impressed. He wondered aloud what kind of example that sets for the grandchildren, which is, to be fair, the exact question every one of those kids' parents is probably also asking.

The charges

She was charged with burglary, petit theft, criminal mischief and contributing to the delinquency of a child, according to reports. The ghost was returned, and the sheriff's office later posted that she had bonded out, with a seasonal warning to keep an eye on your inflatable Santa.

That's four charges for a $15 decoration, which works out to about $3.75 per charge. Not a great exchange rate.

The lesson for the grandkids

Florida's lawn-decoration season runs from roughly September to New Year's, and the inflatables only get bigger from here. Reindeer. Snow globes. A 12-foot Grinch, which honestly would have been more on brand.

She is presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court. The ghost is home, reinflated and, we assume, still a little shaken.
