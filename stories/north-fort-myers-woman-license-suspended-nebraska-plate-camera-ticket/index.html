Florida Woman Loses Her License Over a Miami-Dade Camera Ticket for a Nebraska Plate That Expired in 2003

She hadn't been to Miami-Dade in nearly 20 years and has never been to Nebraska. The camera didn't care.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

North Fort Myers, Lee County · Nov 27, 2026 · 2 min read

The quick take

A North Fort Myers woman learned in September 2026 that her driver's license was suspended over an unpaid 2025 school-zone speeding ticket from a Miami-Dade camera. The citation listed a Nebraska plate that expired in 2003; she says she hasn't been to Miami-Dade in nearly 20 years and has never been to Nebraska. The Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office blamed a vendor error, and the ticket was dismissed, but she spent weeks unable to drive and still had to pay to get her license back.

Florida has a lot of ways to lose your driver's license. Racing on I-75. Driving a golf cart through a Publix. Doing donuts in a Waffle House parking lot. But a North Fort Myers woman found a brand-new one: getting a speeding ticket in a county you haven't visited in nearly two decades, for a car from a state you've never been to.

Her husband found out first. In September 2026 he tried to renew the plates on their vehicle and was told her license was suspended, according to WBBH. She got the news by phone, which is not how anyone wants to learn they're a wanted driver.

The mystery ticket

When she got a copy of the citation, things got stranger. It was a 2025 school-zone speeding violation from Miami-Dade County, recorded by a camera, not an officer. And the plate on the ticket? A Nebraska tag that expired in 2003.

She told WBBH she has never been to Nebraska and doesn't know anyone from there. She also hadn't been to Miami-Dade in nearly 20 years. Experts agree: it is very difficult to speed through a school zone from 150 miles away.

How the ticket found her

Because the ticket went unpaid, the process ground forward on its own. Guessing Headlights, citing Florida law, explained that unpaid camera tickets lead to mailed notices, then a formal citation, and eventually a notice to the state that can trigger a license suspension. Nobody in that chain, apparently, stopped to ask why a Lee County woman was being blamed for a Nebraska car.

The Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office later explained what went wrong. A spokesperson told WBBH it was a vendor user error: the vendor looked up a Florida license plate instead of the Nebraska one, sent it to the state motor vehicle agency, and the state sent back her information. The sheriff's office called it an isolated incident.

Weeks without wheels

She filed a motion to get the ticket dismissed and her license reinstated, then waited. And waited. In Southwest Florida, where a trip to the grocery store can mean a 20-minute drive, she had to rely on other people for rides. "By week 5 — it was no longer cute," she told WBBH.

The ticket was eventually dismissed. But she still had to pay to get her license reinstated, according to both outlets, which is a bit like getting a refund on a sandwich you never ordered and then being charged for the napkin.

The bigger picture

WBBH noted that camera tickets have caused other headaches in the region. Cape Coral's school-zone cameras generated more than 37,000 violation notices from August 2025 to June 2026, and more than 3,500 were dismissed. On Sanibel, a camera vendor refunded more than $130,000 after recording the wrong speeds.

The moral: check your mail, double-check the plate on any ticket you get, and if a camera says you were speeding in a car from Nebraska, you are allowed to be suspicious. The robots are watching. They just aren't always reading carefully.
