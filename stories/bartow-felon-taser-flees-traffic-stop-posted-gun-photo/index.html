Florida Man Allegedly Runs From a Bartow Traffic Stop, Takes a Taser Below the Belt, Keeps Running

Deputies say the 21-year-old felon had also posted a photo of himself with a gun online. Sheriff Grady Judd had thoughts.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Bartow, Polk County · Nov 27, 2026 · 3 min read

The quick take

Polk County deputies say a 21-year-old Bartow man ran from a Nov. 17 traffic stop that started over a badly attached temporary tag, got hit by a Taser in the groin, got back up and kept running before he was caught. Deputies say they found two guns in the car, one reported stolen, and the sheriff says the man, a convicted felon, had posted a photo of himself with a gun online. He faces 10 charges.

Some traffic stops end with a warning. Some end with a ticket. And some, in Polk County, end with a sheriff's press conference and a Thanksgiving roast.

This one started with a temporary license plate that, according to the Polk County Sheriff's Office, simply wasn't attached properly. It ended with 10 charges, a trip to the hospital for medical clearance and a Taser shot to the one place nobody wants a Taser shot.

The stop

According to the arrest affidavit, as reported by FOX 13, deputies pulled over a Toyota sedan near U.S. Highway 60 and Flamingo Drive in Bartow on Nov. 17 over that loose temporary tag. A deputy said he smelled marijuana, then saw the front-seat passenger, a 21-year-old Bartow man, trying to empty a small baggie of something green and leafy.

Deputies told him to step out. Instead, deputies say, he pulled the oldest trick in the book: he pointed off into the distance and asked what happened over there. The deputy looked. The man ran.

It is the classic look-over-there move. It works in cartoons. It works on toddlers. It works, briefly, on deputies, which is not the same thing as working.

The chase (and the Taser)

Deputies say he ran east toward a field along Highway 60, then turned north into the woods. A deputy fired a Taser. The first cartridge missed. The second, according to the affidavit, hit him in the testicles and he went down.

Then, deputies say, he got back up and kept running. Florida has produced a lot of determined men over the years, but this is a special kind of commitment.

Deputies eventually caught him. The affidavit says he fought the handcuffs and struck a deputy on the head hard enough to knock the deputy's hat off.

What was in the car

Back at the Toyota, deputies say they found less than 20 grams of marijuana and a duffel bag behind the driver's seat holding two guns: a 9 mm pistol reported stolen in Winter Haven on Aug. 21, and an AR-style pistol with an extended magazine. Detectives were still checking whether the second gun was stolen, too.

Here's the problem, according to the sheriff's office: the man already has a record, including an August 2024 conviction for possession of ammunition by a convicted felon. In Florida, a felon possessing a firearm is generally a second-degree felony that can bring up to 15 years in prison.

Asked why he ran, deputies say, he told them he figured he was headed to state prison anyway and decided to give it a try. Points for honesty. Fewer points for the plan.

The sheriff weighs in

Sheriff Grady Judd said the man had posted a photo online of himself pointing a gun with another firearm in his lap, and wondered aloud how a convicted felon decides that is a good idea. Then he served up a holiday message, telling the man he was "gonna be eating turkey off a square loaf." (That's jail food, for the uninitiated.)

The charges

He faces 10 charges, including two counts of possession of a firearm by a convicted felon, grand theft of a firearm, battery on a law enforcement officer, resisting an officer with violence, possession of 20 grams or less of cannabis, possession of drug paraphernalia and attempted tampering with evidence. No bond or court date was reported.

So the lesson, once again: attach your temporary tag, don't post gun selfies if you're a felon, and if a deputy is holding a Taser, the woods are not as far away as they look. He is presumed innocent until proven guilty, though he may want to sit down for a while either way.
