Florida Woman Allegedly Rings Up Her Target Haul at Loofah Prices

Bedding, bath products and groceries, all priced like a $2.99 bath sponge. Target staff were not fooled.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Florida · May 15, 2026 · 1 min read

The quick take

A Florida woman is accused of using the barcode from a $2.99 loofah to ring up much pricier items at a Target self-checkout. A store employee watched the whole thing; she left with about $181 in unpaid goods and was stopped.

Nobody walks out of Target spending only what they planned. It's the law of the red bullseye. You go in for toothpaste and come out with a throw pillow, a candle called Coastal Breeze and a decorative bowl you will never use.

One Florida woman, according to reports, found a workaround. It involved a loofah.

The plan

Reports say she loaded up a cart with bedding, bath products and groceries. Then, at self-checkout, she allegedly skipped the real barcodes and scanned a loofah UPC tag priced at $2.99 instead. Fancy sheets? Loofah price. Shampoo? Loofah price. Groceries? You see where this is going.

As a business concept, it's ambitious. If everything in the store costs the same as a loofah, Target becomes the Dollar Tree with better lighting.

The flaw

The flaw in the plan was the people. A Target employee had already spotted her and kept watch as she shopped, according to reports. She also allegedly put some items straight into bags without scanning them at all.

When she walked out with about $181.58 worth of unpaid merchandise, she was brought back to the store's loss-prevention office. Retail workers have seen it all, and they have absolutely seen the loofah trick.

Not the first time

Florida Targets have become a regular stop on the Florida Man circuit. In recent years, deputies around the state have reported shoppers swapping clothing tags for cheaper ones, sticking a pencil-box price on a Lego set and carrying in their own pocketful of barcode stickers. Stores are watching self-checkout more closely than ever.

What happens next

She is presumed innocent unless proven guilty. The loofah, for its part, never asked to be involved,, and would like to go back to its quiet life in the bath aisle.
