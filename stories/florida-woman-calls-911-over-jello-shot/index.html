Florida Woman Calls 911 Three Times Because a Bar Won't Serve Her a Jell-O Shot

Fire, flood, Jell-O shot denial. Only one of those is not an emergency, Marion County deputies would like you to know.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Ocala · May 28, 2026 · 1 min read

The quick take

Marion County deputies say a 68-year-old woman called 911 three times asking for help getting a Jell-O shot from an Ocala bar, then called a fourth time while a deputy was on the way. She's charged with misuse of 911, trespassing and marijuana possession.

There are real emergencies. Car crashes. House fires. A gator in the pool.

And then there's the moment a bar in Ocala tells you no Jell-O shot for you, and you reach for the phone like it's a five-alarm fire.

The calls

According to a Marion County Sheriff's Office arrest affidavit, a 68-year-old woman called 911 in late May because the No Where Bar on Southwest 27th Avenue wouldn't give her a Jell-O shot. She called three times, deputies say, each time asking for a deputy to come help her get alcohol from the bar.

Dispatchers tried calling her back. No luck. So a deputy headed to the bar, and while that deputy was on the way, deputies say she called 911 again. Persistence is a virtue. Just not this kind.

The arrival

When the deputy pulled up, the woman was sitting at a dining table out front. The deputy did not arrive with a Jell-O shot.

Instead she was arrested and taken to the Marion County Jail, charged with misuse of 911 services, trespassing and possession of under 20 grams of marijuana.

Why this one's actually a crime

Florida law says 911 is for emergencies only. Knowingly using it for anything else is a first-degree misdemeanor, which can mean up to a year in jail. Repeat offenders can face more. Every non-emergency call ties up a dispatcher who could be handling a real one.

The lesson

If a bar cuts you off, that's not a crime scene. That's a bartender doing their job. Call a friend, call a ride, or call it a night.

She is presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court. The Jell-O shot, for its part, remains at large.
