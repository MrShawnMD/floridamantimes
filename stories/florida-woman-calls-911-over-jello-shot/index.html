Florida Woman Calls 911 Three Times Because a Bar Won't Serve Her a Jell-O Shot

Fire, flood, Jell-O shot denial. Only one of those is not an emergency, Ocala police would like you to know.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Ocala · Jun 2, 2026 · 1 min read

There are real emergencies. Car crashes. House fires. A gator in the pool. And then there is the moment an Ocala bartender looks you in the eye and says, no more Jell-O shots for you.

Police say one woman put that last one in the first category. When the bar refused to serve her the little wiggly cup of joy, she called 911 about it. Then she called again. Then, in case the first two didn't take, a third time.

Somewhere a 911 dispatcher, juggling the actual problems of Marion County, picked up the phone and got an update on a bar's gelatin policy. Three times.

Officers responded the way officers do. She was charged with offenses that included misusing the 911 system, which, to be fair, is exactly what calling 911 about a Jell-O shot is.

A public service reminder from Florida Man Times: 911 is for emergencies. Getting cut off at the bar is not an emergency. It's a sign. Usually the sign says 'Call an Uber.'

She is presumed innocent unless proven guilty. The Jell-O shot remains at large.
