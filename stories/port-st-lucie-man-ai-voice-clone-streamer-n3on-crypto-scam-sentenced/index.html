Florida Man Gets Nearly 4 Years for Using an AI Clone of a Famous Streamer's Voice to Score $260,000 in Crypto

He signed deals in Kick star N3on's name and sent a fake voice memo as proof. The meme coin crew paid up.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Port St. Lucie, St. Lucie County · Nov 27, 2026 · 2 min read

The quick take

A 36-year-old Port St. Lucie man was sentenced to 47 months in federal prison after prosecutors say he posed as Kick streamer N3on, signed deals in his name and sent an AI-generated recording of the streamer's voice to the organizers of a meme coin project. They sent about $260,000 in cryptocurrency to accounts he controlled. He pleaded guilty to money laundering and aggravated identity theft, and the U.S. Attorney's Office in South Florida announced the sentence on Nov. 24.

Once upon a time, a Florida con man needed a fake mustache and a good accent. Now he just needs a laptop, a famous voice he doesn't own and a group of crypto investors who really, really want a celebrity endorsement.

Federal prosecutors say a 36-year-old Port St. Lucie man had all three. It worked to the tune of about $260,000. Then it worked out to 47 months in federal prison.

The voice

According to the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of Florida, as reported by Decrypt and Dexerto, the man set up online accounts made to look like they belonged to N3on, a popular streamer on the platform Kick, and his representatives.

Posing as the streamer's camp, he reached out to the organizers of a cryptocurrency meme coin project. Prosecutors say he signed agreements using N3on's identity. And when the organizers wanted proof, he sent them an AI-generated recording that mimicked the streamer's voice and confirmed he was on board.

Mock-serious reminder to anyone in crypto: if your big-name endorsement arrives as a voice memo from someone you've never met, it's possible you are not, in fact, partnering with a celebrity.

The money

Believing the deal was real, prosecutors say, the organizers sent about $260,000 in cryptocurrency to accounts the man controlled. He then converted a big chunk of it into U.S. dollars through transactions designed to hide where the money came from and who owned it, and spent it on himself. The press release did not name the meme coin project.

Acting U.S. Attorney Yara Lorenzo Klukas said the man "exploited artificial intelligence to manipulate the trust of unsuspecting victims." The FBI's Miami field office investigated.

The sentence

The man pleaded guilty to money laundering and aggravated identity theft. U.S. District Judge Melissa Damian sentenced him to 47 months in federal prison and ordered him to pay restitution to the victims. Prosecutors announced the sentence on Nov. 24.

For context, aggravated identity theft is the federal charge that comes with a mandatory two years tacked on to the sentence for the underlying crime, and money laundering can carry up to 20 years. In other words, borrowing someone else's identity is one of the more expensive things you can do with a microphone.

Why it matters

AI voice cloning used to be a party trick. Now it's showing up in courtrooms. Scammers have used fake voices of grandkids, bosses and bank officers to talk people out of money, and this case adds a new twist: a fake influencer endorsing a coin built on hype.

If you're ever offered a deal on the strength of a celebrity's voice note, call the celebrity's real people. Not the number in the voice note. The real ones.

What's next

He heads to federal prison, where the only streaming will be the water from the sink. His case is closed with a guilty plea, so the jokes here are aimed at the plan, not a pending trial. And N3on, for the record, never signed anything. His voice just got a side gig without him.
