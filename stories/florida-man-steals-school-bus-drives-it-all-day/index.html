Florida Man Steals a School Bus, Drives It All Day Because He Figured He Could

No kids. No route. No plan. Just a big yellow dream and the open roads of Miami-Dade.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Miami-Dade County · Nov 6, 2026 · 2 min read

The quick take

Deputies say a 23-year-old Miami Gardens man took a 2001 school bus from a private bus company's lot, kept it for about 24 hours, then led them on a slow-motion chase that ended at a dead end. His explanation, per the arrest report: he thought he could get away with it.

Some men buy a sports car when they need a little freedom. Others take up golf. One 23-year-old from Miami Gardens skipped both and, deputies say, went straight for a school bus.

Not a minivan. Not a golf cart. A full-size, 2001 International 3000 school bus. The vehicle you can spot from space, the one that comes with its own flashing stop sign.

The heist

On a Wednesday in early November, someone slipped into the gated yard of Little Angels Bus Service in the unincorporated Gladeview area of Miami-Dade and drove off in one of its buses. When the company pulled the security video, the owner didn't need a sketch artist. He told deputies he recognized the driver, and that the same man had taken a bus from that same lot before.

At some point you stop being a thief and start being a regular.

The chase (please read at 25 mph)

Around 6:45 the next morning, a Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office detective in an unmarked car spotted the bus rolling north through northwest Miami-Dade. A marked deputy tried to pull it over. The bus kept going. Deputies turned off their sirens and followed at a safe distance, tried a second stop, and the bus kept going again.

Then Florida's street grid did what the sirens could not. The bus turned onto Northwest 73rd Terrace, hit a dead end, and that was the end of the field trip. He was the only one aboard.

The explanation(s)

At the station, deputies say he first told them another man had threatened his family and forced him to steal the bus for parts. Detectives then asked a fair follow-up: why drive it around for roughly 24 hours? According to the arrest report, he said he took it "because he thought he could get away with it."

Bold strategy, for a getaway vehicle painted the brightest color known to science and built to be noticed by every driver on the road.

What happens next

Deputies also say his driver's license had been suspended since August 2024. He faces felony charges of grand theft of a vehicle, burglary of an unoccupied structure and fleeing and eluding police, plus misdemeanor reckless driving and driving with a suspended license. A judge set bond at $5,500 and ordered him to stay away from the bus company.

He is presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court. The bus, we assume, is just glad to be home, and the bus company may want to look into a bigger lock.
