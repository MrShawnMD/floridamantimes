Welcome to Florida: New Arrivals Find an 8-Foot Gator Parked in Their Driveway

The couple just moved to Manatee County. The gator was here first, and he'd like you to know it.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Palmetto, Manatee County · Aug 23, 2026 · 1 min read

The quick take

A couple who moved from Pennsylvania to Palmetto found an 8-foot, 2-inch alligator under their carport next to the truck and golf cart. A pool noodle did not work. An FWC-contracted trapper did.

Moving to Florida comes with a to-do list. Get a Florida driver's license. Learn what a Pub Sub is. Find out what your HOA thinks about your mailbox.

For Allan and Sandy Hofmann, who moved to Palmetto from Pennsylvania in March, the list got a surprise new item in late August: evict the dinosaur.

The morning routine

Allan already knew the neighborhood had gators. He'd seen them before, so he'd built a habit: every morning, before walking the dog, he checked under the truck and the golf cart. Smart. Very Florida. Highly recommended.

On Sunday, Aug. 23, he did the check, walked the dog and came home to find that the gator had simply waited until he left. It was stretched out on the concrete under the carport, right between the truck and the golf cart, like it had a reserved spot.

The pool noodle plan

Allan told reporters he climbed up on the golf cart and tapped the gator on the head with a pink pool noodle, hoping it would take the hint. The gator took no hint. It hissed. It stayed.

Sandy put it at 300 to 350 pounds. It measured 8 feet, 2 inches, and it left claw marks in the driveway as a souvenir.

The professionals

The couple called 911, and a trapper working with the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission came out and captured the gator alive. The timing tracks: late August into early September is when Florida gator eggs hatch, and gators are on the move all summer.

If a gator shows up at your house

Don't poke it, noodle it or feed it. Keep kids and pets back and call FWC's free Nuisance Alligator Hotline at 866-392-4286 (866-FWC-GATOR). The state sends a contracted trapper, and it doesn't cost the homeowner anything.

The Hofmanns say they hope newcomers learn from their story. Welcome to Florida, neighbors. Check under the car twice.
