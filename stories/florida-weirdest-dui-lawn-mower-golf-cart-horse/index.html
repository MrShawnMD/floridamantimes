Lawn Mowers, Golf Carts and a Horse: Florida's Weirdest Ways to Get a DUI

In the Sunshine State, the vehicle is optional. The arrest is not.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Statewide · Nov 24, 2026 · 3 min read

The quick take

In Florida you can get a DUI on almost anything with an engine, and deputies have tried it on a horse. A roundup of the state's strangest impaired-driving arrests: a lawn mower on a toll road, a golf cart in The Villages area, 34 open hard seltzers and a mower carrying a bag of suspected meth.

Most people think of a DUI as something that happens in a car. Florida thinks of that as a starting point.

In the Sunshine State, deputies and troopers have arrested people for driving impaired on riding mowers, golf carts and, at least once, a horse. Here are some of the strangest real cases, and the one serious lesson hiding inside all of them.

The lawn mower on the toll road

On a Friday morning in July 2025, drivers on the Suncoast Parkway started calling in something you don't see every day: a man on a riding lawn mower, weaving southbound on a highway built for 70 mph. Traffic cameras tracked the mower as it rolled from Citrus County into Hernando County, and Florida Highway Patrol troopers stopped it just south of the U.S. 98 exit.

Troopers said the 38-year-old rider showed signs of impairment, refused field sobriety exercises and was arrested for DUI. He was out on $500 bond that afternoon. The lawn, presumably, still needs mowing.

The mower with a side of meth

In August 2026, a Dixie County narcotics deputy pulled over a man riding a red lawn mower down a county road in Old Town. The reason for the stop was simple: a standard lawn mower isn't street legal. Then the deputy said he smelled marijuana coming from the man, the mower or both, and found a bag between the rider's feet with suspected methamphetamine, marijuana and pills inside.

Pro tip from absolutely everyone: if you're going to break one law, don't do it while visibly breaking a second one.

The golf cart that drifted

Golf carts are the official vehicle of Central Florida retirement, and yes, they count. One December night in Lady Lake, a Sumter County deputy watched a golf cart roll through a flashing red light and drift over the double yellow line, more than once. The 71-year-old driver admitted to a couple of drinks, then told the deputy, "You have to admit, I'm not that drunk."

The deputy did not admit that. The man was charged with DUI and released from jail in under 12 hours.

34 White Claws, one passenger seat

In June 2026, a Florida Highway Patrol trooper on I-75 stopped a 33-year-old driver after he blew past at well over the speed limit. Troopers reported slurred speech and bloodshot eyes, and they counted 34 open cans of hard seltzer on the passenger seat, partly hidden under a T-shirt. His breath tests came back at .177 and .166, more than double Florida's .08 limit. He has pleaded not guilty.

The horse named Bo Duke

And then there's the classic. Back in 2017, Polk County deputies found a 53-year-old woman riding her horse down a busy Lakeland road, apparently confused. She was charged with DUI and animal neglect, and the horse, named Bo Duke, was taken to the sheriff's livestock facility. Sheriff Grady Judd famously said that in Polk County you can get a DUI in a car, a truck or on a horse. Defense lawyers have pointed out the law is written for vehicles. Bo Duke has declined to comment.

The part that isn't a joke

Here's the serious bit. Florida's DUI law covers anyone impaired by alcohol or drugs while driving or in actual control of a vehicle, and that includes golf carts, mopeds and riding mowers on a public road. The legal limit is .08. A first conviction can bring fines, a license suspension and up to six months in jail, and much more if someone gets hurt.

None of these stories ended in a crash. Plenty of others do. If you've been drinking, hand off the keys, call a ride or stay put. If you see a driver who looks impaired, pull over safely and call 911 or *FHP (*347).

Everyone mentioned here is presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court. The lawn mowers are all innocent, full stop.
