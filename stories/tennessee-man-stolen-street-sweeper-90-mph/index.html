Tennessee Man Allegedly Takes a Stolen Street Sweeper for a 90 MPH Thanksgiving Spin

Nothing says holiday spirit like a curb-cleaning truck doing double the speed limit at 12:30 a.m.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Mt. Juliet, Tennessee · Nov 27, 2026 · 2 min read

The quick take

Around 12:30 a.m. on Thanksgiving, Nov. 26, a Mt. Juliet, Tennessee, police officer spotted a pickup-based street sweeper going more than 90 mph in a 45 mph zone on Lebanon Road. The driver took off, the officer didn't chase, and a Wilson County deputy later spotted the sweeper heading toward Lebanon. Police say the sweeper had been stolen in Nashville; a 24-year-old La Vergne man faces felony evading and other charges.

Florida has spent years as the undisputed champion of strange vehicles at strange speeds. Golf carts on the interstate. Lawn mowers at the drive-thru. Then Tennessee showed up on Thanksgiving morning and said: hold my brush.

At about 12:30 a.m. on Thursday, Nov. 26, a Mt. Juliet police officer on Lebanon Road saw something nobody expects to see in the rearview mirror, or anywhere: a pickup truck-based street sweeper going more than 90 mph, according to WSMV, FOX 17 and Rutherford Source. The speed limit there is 45.

The getaway vehicle nobody picks

Street sweepers are many things. Helpful. Loud. Slow, generally, by design. They are built to crawl along curbs eating leaves and bottle caps, not to compete in drag races. Driving one at 90 mph is like taking a Roomba to a NASCAR qualifier.

The officer tried to pull it over, and the driver kept going, police said. Because of road conditions, the officer chose not to chase. Instead, Mt. Juliet police shared the vehicle's description with other agencies. Mock-serious note: there are not many pickup-based street sweepers out joyriding at half past midnight on Thanksgiving. It is not a hard vehicle to describe.

The second sighting

Sure enough, a Wilson County Sheriff's deputy spotted the sweeper still on Lebanon Road, heading east toward Lebanon. This time the driver stopped, and Mt. Juliet officers took him into custody, according to the reports.

That's when the story got better. Police say the sweeper had been stolen in Nashville. And the driver, a 24-year-old La Vergne man, was already wanted by Nashville authorities, with FOX 17 reporting a warrant for aggravated reckless endangerment from Metro Nashville police.

The charges

He faces a felony evading charge, all three outlets reported. FOX 17 reported a theft charge as well; WSMV and Rutherford Source said the stolen-vehicle report led to additional charges without naming them. No bond or court date was reported.

Mt. Juliet police credited the officer's judgment and the department's partnership with the Wilson County Sheriff's Office for catching a wanted man and recovering the stolen vehicle without a high-speed chase. Nobody was reported hurt.

Florida's official reaction

We have seen a lot of things down here. But a street sweeper at twice the speed limit, on Thanksgiving, while wanted? Tennessee, that was a Florida move, and we respect the hustle. The man is presumed innocent until proven guilty. The sweeper, at least, is presumably back to cleaning curbs at a more reasonable pace.
