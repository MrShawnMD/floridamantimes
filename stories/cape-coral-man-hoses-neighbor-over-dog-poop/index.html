Florida Man Allegedly Hoses Down His Neighbor Over Dog Poop on His Lawn

Four dogs, one lawn, one high-pressure nozzle and a phone that will never turn on again.

FM
Florida Man Times Desk

Cape Coral, Lee County · Nov 24, 2026 · 2 min read

The quick take

A 67-year-old Cape Coral man was arrested after police say he sprayed a neighbor with a high-pressure garden hose because her four dogs had left waste on his lawn. She told police it went on for about three minutes and wrecked her $250 phone. He faces misdemeanor battery and criminal mischief charges.

Every Florida neighborhood has a cold war. Usually it's fought with HOA complaints, passive-aggressive yard signs and the occasional pointed stare over a hedge. In one Cape Coral neighborhood, police say, it was fought with a garden hose.

According to an arrest report, it started on a Sunday when a woman was walking her four dogs past a 67-year-old man's house. He was not a fan of what the dogs had been leaving on his lawn.

The spray

Police say the man turned his garden hose, fitted with a high-pressure nozzle, on the woman. When officers asked why, the report says he told them he did it to let her know how it feels. Which is a bold philosophy of justice and, for the record, not one recognized by Florida courts.

The woman told police he sprayed her for about three minutes, mostly in the face, while she tried to film the whole thing on her phone. Three minutes is a long time. That's a full Publix checkout line on a Sunday after church.

Two versions of events

Not everyone saw it the same way. A witness told police the woman just got a little wet when the man sprayed the dogs off his property. So depending on who you ask, this was either a three-minute face-blast or a light misting with some collateral damage.

Either way, the phone lost. The woman told police her phone, worth about $250, now has water under the screen and won't turn on. Somewhere, the cloud backup is the real hero of this story.

The charges

He was charged with misdemeanor battery and misdemeanor criminal mischief, according to Gulf Coast News. Records show he was booked into the Lee County Jail, released on a $1,500 bond, and has a court date set for Dec. 15.

A word to both sides of the hedge

Florida has plenty of ways to settle a lawn dispute: talk to your neighbor, call code enforcement, put up a polite little sign that says "Please pick up after your pet." A pressure nozzle aimed at a human face is not on the list. Neither, for the dog walkers, is leaving the evidence behind. Bring the bags.

He is presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court. The lawn, we're told, is very well watered.
